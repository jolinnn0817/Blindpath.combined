BlindPath

Find connected, mapped tactile paving for pedestrian journeys.

A mapped tactile route is not a verified safe route. Mapping can be incomplete or outdated. This app cannot detect obstacles or traffic signals. Use your usual mobility aid and check crossings independently.

Search, then select a result. If a building is missing, tap its pedestrian entrance on the map. Coordinate input is also accepted.

Choose a starting point and destination.

Mapped tactile route

Amber line: OpenStreetMap ways tagged tactile_paving=yes. This is a mapping claim, not an inspection. Connections across unmapped gaps are never invented.

Spoken location progress

This announces proximity to the mapped path and remaining mapped distance. It does not provide left/right instructions, confirm which side of the road you are on, or tell you when to cross. GPS cannot reliably distinguish a narrow tactile strip.

Location tracking is off.

Missing tactile-path data?

A normal walking route does not prove tactile paving exists. If no mapped connection is found, BlindPath stops instead of substituting a general walking route. A limited search area can also miss a longer connection.

For a UM pilot, survey the actual paving, junctions, entrances and crossings with campus accessibility staff and visually impaired users. Add accurate tactile tags and connected geometry to OpenStreetMap, or supply a separately surveyed dataset. Do not mark paths as tactile without checking them.
